When replying to a **sponsor or artifact-owner who already amplifies you** (Ian Boston cites Pedro's blog repeatedly), the reply should **validate + name the gap as shared + hand the question back** — keep them the architect. Do **not** re-explain the point they just raised back to them, and do **not** position your own thesis as "the real argument" that answers their concern. That reads as a subtle one-up / competing with the sponsor, even when the content is correct.

Source: 2026-06-02 skillshare↔skills.sh thread. Ian raised the npx/registry supply-chain risk. Pedro's first reply (sent) answered-over: re-explained the risk + asserted "that's the real argument for [my git-native declaration approach]." Pedro caught it after sending ("c'est pas un tackle à Ian ça?"). The lighter version would have been: agree on the risk, name trust/provenance as the unsolved part *for all of these*, ask "where would you want that to sit for us."

**On damage control:** once an answer-over reply is sent, do **not** send a "what I meant was…" follow-up. Litigating your own reply draws attention to a problem the other person may not have registered and reads insecure ([[feedback_dont_litigate_prior_replies]]). Course-correct **forward on their next turn** — hand back the architect seat with a question/build — only escalate to an explicit hand-back if they actually react negatively.

**Why:** Ian is the architecture sponsor and is semi-disengaging; competing with him (even subtly) risks the amplifier. Co-authoring keeps him engaged and keeps Pedro's lane validated by him rather than asserted by Pedro.

**How to apply:** drafting any reply to Ian/Bertrand/Loni on a thread they own — check the last beat. If it ends by asserting Pedro's answer, rewrite to end on a shared-gap observation + a question back. Pairs with [[feedback_additive_not_corrective]] and the leadership entry "Co-Author With the Owner."
