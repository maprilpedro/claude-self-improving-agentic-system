When drafting a message Pedro will send (Slack, email), it must sound like **him**, not like a polished native-English AI.

**Rules:**
1. **Plain English at his level.** No native-speaker idioms ("snagging," "the odd prune," "keep coming back to," "pull together"). Use words he'd actually write ("the part I'm still thinking about," "fill up with," "a regular prune," "put together something concrete").
   - **No clever metaphors or strategy-speak either.** 2026-06-01 he rejected "rails" and "own the rails that make self-curation still feel like one product" — too metaphorical/abstract for him. Say it literally: "the surface decides its own skills … I'm not the one choosing them, I own the definition and the check." If a noun is a metaphor he wouldn't reach for ("rails," "the plumbing," "the moat"), swap it for the plain thing it stands for.
2. **No meta-narration of the move.** Never write "I'll bring a proposal rather than a question" — just make the point. Narrating the move is the loudest AI tell.
3. **Don't recite the other person's position back to them.** React to it; they already know it.
   - **Don't open with a recap/validation of what they said either** (2026-06-23, Guliz rail thread; flagged AGAIN 2026-06-24, Ian overlap thread). The Guliz draft started "Agreed on building our own components, that is the V1 lesson"; the Ian draft started "Agreed, static alone is incomplete, the correctness signal is the empirical one" = both restate the other's own point back. Pedro: *"tu repetes toujours beaucoup ce que dit [l'autre]… je ne fais pas ca en vrai"* / *"arrête de répéter ce que disent les slack, c'est pas humain."* **Even a one-line concession that paraphrases their point counts as recap — cut it.** Lead straight with **Pedro's move/action** ("I'll run the empirical side on EPA traffic… and bring you numbers"), no "Agreed, X" preamble. At most a 3-4 word bare agree ("Fully agree Guliz"), then substance. Same for restating a question before answering it. The recap is invisible to me but glaring to him as not-his-words.
   - **Don't lean on citing third parties either** (2026-06-17). Minimize naming/quoting other people in his drafts ("Bertrand raised it on the COA thread," etc.). If a reference is needed, use a short fragment, not a full attributing sentence. Make the point his own. He'd rather flag a gap directly ("credit/usage needs an owner") than hang it on someone else's quote. Crediting a senior can stay verbal/out-of-band, not baked into the text.
4. **Short and clipped** (Slack register), not balanced essay prose with every clause weighted.
5. **No em-dashes** (his global writing rule).
6. **Match the recipient's register.** Default-clipped is for execs/seniors; with a peer he is familiar with, go familiar — mirror their opener (Philippe: "Hello l'ami" → match the warmth) while keeping the substance firm. He sets the register ("sois plus familier, je le serai avec lui"); when he flags it, warm the wording without softening the line. A too-formal draft to a buddy reads as not-him just as much as an idiom does.

He flagged this twice in one session (the "rather than a question" meta-line and the word "snagging"), asked for a *more familiar* draft to a peer (2026-05-28, Philippe), rejected metaphor/strategy-speak ("rails", 2026-06-01), and flagged recap-of-the-other-party repeatedly (2026-06-23 Guliz, and again 2026-06-24 Ian: *"c'est pas humain"*) — register, concreteness, and not-reciting are all part of "sounds like him," not just vocabulary level. **🟢 Calibration #8 (2026-07-22, the check-in / review blocks in EN — he caught a hedge word I had not flagged as one):**
- **🔴 IN ENGLISH EXEC WRITING, "CONFIDENT" IS A HEDGE WORD MORE OFTEN THAN A STRENGTH WORD.** I drafted *"AEM did not migrate this year, and that is the outcome I am most confident about."* He asked what "confident about" meant, and he was right to. It is ambiguous across four readings (sure it happened / sure it was right / sure it will hold / I rank it first), and native readers hear the register of managed doubt — *"I'm confident we'll ship"* is read by everyone as *"we might not ship."* **Fix: state the fact flat and cut the self-commentary.** *"AEM was not migrated to Coworker in July. That was deliberate."* **Announcing confidence is less confident than asserting.**
- **Put conviction AFTER the evidence, never before.** Assertion → proof → then the line where you stand behind it. And **once per document** — I had proposed "I would make the same call again" as the closer for two different blocks; repeated, it becomes a tic and both lose weight.
- **This is a non-native-register trap, not a voice preference.** He writes review and status content in EN where a word that feels neutral to him reads as weak to the audience. **When drafting his EN exec prose, sweep for the hedge family — confident, fairly, quite, I believe, I would like to think, hopefully, should — and either cut or replace with a flat declarative.** Same instinct as the Calibration #4 aphorism sweep, opposite direction: there I was too punchy, here I was too soft.
- **He also caught a self-audit gap.** He asked "des alternatives ?" rather than accepting the phrasing — **treat any question he asks about a single word as a signal the word is wrong, not as a request for a definition.** [[feedback_edit_the_span_not_the_artifact]] applies: fix that phrase, not the block.

**How to apply:** after drafting, reread each line as *"would Pedro write this exact word?"* and strip anything above his register. Hedge situational claims in group emails, keep asks assertive, offer the manager a chime-in line when announcing calls, default design leadership to direct-recipient, sweep single-word metaphors, state wants not vetoes, no priority labels, footnote the receipts.
