**Rule.** When Pedro's reply on a thread didn't land the way he intended (received as data-dump, observation, deflection), the recovery move is **forward-framed clarification**, NOT backward-framed defense.

**Wrong:** *"To clarify, what I wrote earlier already implied that…"* — litigates the prior message. Burns political capital arguing with the audience's read.

**Right:** *"To clarify the earlier point — [forward-framed status]. So splitting cleanly: [your lane] / [other person's lane]."* — moves the conversation forward.

**Why:** Senior leaders (Bertrand, Loni, JM, Conrad) read at speed. If they read your reply as "doesn't have it" and the next move is to assign you the work, defending the original reply means *arguing with their read*. That's the wrong fight. Their read becomes the new ground truth — work from there.

**Trigger example (2026-05-07 NYL / TBYB thread):** Pedro's first reply was data-only ("Raul maintains, data 3 weeks old, not aware of other way"). Bertrand assigned. Pedro considered defending the original ("I implied ownership"). Right move was forward-framed: *"To clarify — Raul owns categorization, refresh in flight. Splitting with Yanira: she leads process, I cover data freshness."* Same content, no litigation.

**Heuristic:**
- If your recovery sentence starts with *"Actually I meant…"* or *"To clarify what I wrote…"* — pause.
- Replace with *"To update the thread —"* or *"Status:"* or *"+1 to [other person's plan]."*
- Anchor on what's true NOW, not on what you meant THEN.

**Sizing:** recovery should be 4-6 sentences max. Anything longer reads as defensive and creates new openings for pushback. Send and move on.
