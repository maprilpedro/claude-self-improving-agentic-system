When drafting a message Pedro will send in his own voice (especially to a senior like Bertrand), every technical claim must be either sourced or explicitly marked as inference / to-confirm. Never assert something as settled when it isn't. And do not fuse two distinct systems or concepts into one continuous claim.

**Why:** Pedro-voice drafts go out under his name ([[feedback_keep_claude_private]], [[feedback_draft_in_pedros_voice]]), so any imprecision I introduce becomes Pedro's exposure to leadership, not mine. Concrete miss 2026-06-16: I wrote Bertrand's reply that (a) blurred the skill/extension bridges (Carsten/Sergiu) with the UI renderers (AEMAGT-2140 / Josh's Canonical UI) as if one continuous thing, and (b) implied renderer continuity was in hand without confirming it. Bertrand had asked the precise question "do the 1.0 renderers work as is in Coworker?" — Varun then answered the opposite (no, the UI protocol differs, they must be ported). Pedro caught that I, not he, authored the imprecise claim.

**How to apply:** before putting a technical statement in a Pedro-voice draft, ask "is this sourced, or am I inferring?" If inferring, mark it ("from what I've seen", "to confirm with X") or leave it out. Keep distinct objects distinct — bridges ≠ renderers, skills ≠ UI protocol (extends [[feedback_dont_conflate_pattern_with_object]]). When Pedro asks "was that true?", own the part I authored rather than framing it as his. Mirrors [[feedback_confirm_ask_before_producing]] (mark inferences vs sourced in reply drafts).
