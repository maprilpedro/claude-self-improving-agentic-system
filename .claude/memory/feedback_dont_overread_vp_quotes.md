When senior leaders (Bertrand, Loni, JM) drop a line that sounds promotion-grade ("North Star architecture for Pedro", "this is Pedro's question to own"), parse the sentence carefully before treating it as a Pedro-as-author signal. The grammatical subject matters.

**Concrete example.** 2026-05-12 P42 transcript, Bertrand 6:30: *"this looks like a new North Star architecture for Pedro."* Speaker = Bertrand. Subject of "this" = the architecture Ian Boston just articulated (skill portability, open source, context API). Beneficiary = Pedro. **Author = Ian.** Ian self-assigned the one-pager. My first read promoted Pedro to "North Star architect," which would have shipped wrong into the May 11 deck and into Promotion Strategy v1.

**Why.** Pedro's actual lane in this conversation = PM operator / liaison / portfolio reporter — owns substrate + distribution, not architecture. Misattributing authorship to Pedro:
- Hands Ian's deliverable to Pedro on paper, creating delivery risk if Ian's one-pager slips.
- Reads as overreach if it ever lands in front of Ian, Conrad, or Bertrand verbatim.
- Inflates promotion narrative on a quote that doesn't support it, and weakens stronger real signals (H-005, AEM-AO liaison naming, three-tier reporting ownership).

**How to apply.**
- For every "promotion-grade" verbatim, identify (a) grammatical subject, (b) who acted / will act on it, (c) who benefits / consumes it. Pedro can sit in any of those three slots — they are not the same.
- "For Pedro" / "to Pedro" / "with Pedro" ≠ "by Pedro." Audit the preposition.
- When in doubt, ask before writing the quote into Promotion Strategy or a deck. Pedro will redirect fast if the read is wrong — cheap cost.
- Strong-signal quotes still exist (Bertrand naming Pedro AEM-AO PM liaison April 14, Trent labeling AEM Context "North Star question" April 29). Don't dilute those by inflating weaker ones.
