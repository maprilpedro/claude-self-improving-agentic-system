# Surface the Risk Before Leadership Has to Ask

_Section: Operating Discipline — part of `leadership/`; router = README.md._
- **Date identified**: 2026-04-01
- **Source**: Ian Boston's data compliance response; Pedro's escalation to Bertrand April 1.
- **Pattern**: Ian flagged two confirmed legal risks (data residency, data governance deposition) that no one else had raised. Pedro's move was to escalate to Bertrand before Bertrand encountered it through another channel. This is a Senior Director behavior — surfacing a problem before it surfaces itself.
- **Why it matters**: When leadership discovers a risk on their own or through someone else, the PM is reactive. When the PM surfaces it first — with a diagnosis and a proposed path — they are the person who saw it coming. That's the difference between being reported to and being relied on.
- **How to execute**: Don't just raise the problem. Come with a proposed path and a framing. "Here's what Ian found. Here's how bad it could be. Here's what I think we do short-term and long-term. I need your read before I move." That's not reporting up — that's leading across.
- **The failure mode**: Sitting on risk information because it's uncomfortable to bring, or because it's not "officially your problem." If it touches your product, it's yours to raise.
- **Reinforced 2026-06-22 (the forced-deadline escalation)**: AEM was handed Anjul's ~July 15 cutover (AI Assistant off, Coworker rail on) that AEM could not meet. Two sharpenings on *how* to escalate a deadline you can't hit, both learned by iterating the email to Bertrand:
  - **Lead with a recommendation, not a request for relief.** "I'd like us to push the date until we're ready" reads as a capacity complaint and is open-ended (a VP red flag). "I recommend we move AEM by readiness, phase the agents one by one, and keep customers on the AI Assistant until their agent has landed" is a leader proposing a path. Same facts, opposite altitude.
  - **Reframe the miss as shared platform-readiness, not your capacity.** The strong move was showing the date doesn't hold for *anyone*: the platform owner himself (Josh) said July 15 isn't realistic, the rail crashes on rich output today, server-side rendering is still a proposal, there's no Spectrum catalog. The deadline fails on the platform you depend on, not just on your team. That converts "AEM is behind" into "this date was never real," which is far harder to refuse and doesn't spend your credibility.
  - **Exec summary must be self-contained.** The bottom-line block has to define the date and what it means before using it (what July 15 *is*: AIA off, Coworker rail on), then the problem, then the ask. A VP reading only those three lines should understand the whole thing.
