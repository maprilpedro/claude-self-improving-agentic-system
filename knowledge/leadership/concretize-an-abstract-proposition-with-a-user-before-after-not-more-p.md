# Concretize an Abstract Proposition With a User Before/After, Not More Prose

_Section: Operating at Senior Director Level — part of `leadership/`; router = README.md._

- **Date promoted**: 2026-06-08 (2 independent observations).
- **Source**: Two senior reviewers, different roles, same gap on the same architecture propositions (consistency layer + there-waiting). Silvia Mulet Ferre (Adobe Design), "I'm a bit lost" twice on the consistency story (2026-06-03). Bertrand de Coatpont (Sr Director, Pedro's manager), "j'ai de la peine à concrétiser les 2 propositions" (2026-06-08) — and the clean contrast: Bertrand *liked* the concrete harness-varieties slide and could not concretize the *abstract* propositions in the same sitting.
- **The rule**: an abstract strategic/architecture proposition does not land with senior people through more prose, more precision, or a sharper definition. It lands through **a concrete user before/after** (a named persona crossing the real scenario, what they experience today vs after) **plus the named artifacts that already exist** (pages, schemas, a test — receipts, not concepts) **plus the mechanism pushed to the end** (how it actually works, step by step). Sophistication of the audience does not remove the need — a VP and a principal designer needed the same scaffolding a customer would.
- **Why it works**: the before/after gives the idea a body the listener can re-tell ("three products become one Adobe"); the named artifacts kill the "is this just a concept?" doubt (especially for a requirements-first manager); the end-to-end mechanism answers "but how, technically?" without forcing the PM to bluff (mark [real spec] vs [to define] — see *own the requirement, not the implementation*).
- **PM Application (Director→SD)**: Pedro's visibility gap is that his propositions are strong but read abstract. When a proposition fails to land, the reflex is to explain more; the move is to **drop a scene + show the receipts**. A Senior Director makes the abstract concrete for the room; reciting the definition again reads as not having the answer. The before/after is a reusable asset — built once for Silvia, it served Bertrand unchanged.
- **Mechanism**: scene (persona, today-column vs after-column) → artifacts (named, with status + where they live) → the end-to-end "how it works" → one retellable line. Offer the axes scoped ("experience / process / artifacts — which is fuzzy?") rather than asking the blank "what don't you get" (the scoped ask lets the reviewer point; it also signals you already have all three concrete).
- **Boundary**: not every idea needs a before/after — a one-step ask or a known pattern does not. This is for *abstract propositions that keep failing to land* despite the audience being capable. Two instances so far (both on the One-AEM propositions); watch whether it generalizes beyond this deck.

- **Outcome confirmation (2026-06-10 — same artifact chain, not a new instance): the deck built through this rule LANDED.** The Bucharest keynote (the artifact the two failing-to-land instances forced into before/after scenes, named artifacts, process slides) was delivered 2026-06-10 to ~56 mixed attendees — **feedback excellent, from Bertrand (the reviewer who originally couldn't concretize) plus 10+ attendees** (per Pedro, verbatims to collect). Outcome-validation that the concretization work moved the result, not just the review; the "watch whether it generalizes beyond this deck" question stays open.
- **Refinement (2026-06-08, same Bertrand/deck — sharpening, not a new instance): a process beats a taxonomy, and define-or-cut the jargon.** Two techniques that made the abstract land: (1) **turn a static taxonomy into a numbered process.** Bertrand could not concretize a *list of parts* ("the definition" = a chat component + six dimensions, a taxonomy) but the *same content as a who-does-what-in-what-order process* (agree → write down → teams check → drift caught) landed immediately — a process carries agency and sequence a list does not. When a "here are the N components" slide won't land, rewrite it as "here is how it gets built and used." (2) **De-jargon by define-inline or cut.** A load-bearing term that recurs across slides (e.g. "dimensions", also on the frame slide) gets *defined inline on first use* with concrete examples, not dropped (dropping it costs cross-slide consistency); a pure-wrapper term gets replaced. And **cut any step whose purpose you can't state in one breath** — Bertrand's "ça sert à quoi?" on the surface-map step was the test; its coverage role folded into the next step + the closer and the process got tighter. Audience bar: a mixed room + a requirements-first manager need the verb (the process), the defined term (no orphan jargon), and no dead beats.
