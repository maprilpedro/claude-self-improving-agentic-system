# Name the Shape of a Non-Result, or It Defaults to Failure

Date identified: 2026-07-22
Source: rewriting Pedro's 2026 check-in blocks. Derived from applying it to three separate non-results in one document, not from a single observed field event. Flagged as derived rather than observed, on purpose.

## The problem

Reviews are written in the language of things that happened. When the honest answer to "what did you achieve" is that something did *not* happen, most people do one of two things. They stay vague, which reads as evasion. Or they dress adjacent work up as the missing delivery, which reads fine until someone checks, and then it discredits everything around it.

Both fail for the same reason. A non-result with no stated shape defaults to the reader's cheapest interpretation, which is failure or drift.

But non-results are not all the same thing, and the differences are large.

## Three shapes, and how each is claimed

**Prevented loss.** Something bad did not happen because you stopped it. This is only an achievement if two conditions hold. You can name the counterfactual concretely, and you can show you caused the prevention. If either is missing it is luck being narrated as strategy, and one question exposes it. When both hold, this is among the strongest claims available, because prevented losses are invisible by nature and the person who makes them visible is the only one who can be credited.

**Deliberate null.** The metric you own did not move, and you chose that. The claim is not "here is why it didn't move", which is explanation. The claim is "I decided it would not move this year, and here is what I bought instead". Explanation puts you in the position of a person things happen to. The decision framing puts a choice, and therefore a chooser, in the sentence.

**Blocked versus not-got-to.** When several commitments slipped, resist the single blanket cause. Separate the ones that were genuinely unbuildable from the ones you simply did not reach. Almost nobody does this, because admitting the second category feels worse than a uniform excuse. It is the opposite. A uniform excuse is read as an excuse. A precise split is read as someone who knows their own book, and it makes the blocked half credible, which the blanket version never is.

## Why this is a seniority marker specifically

Directors report what happened to their area. Senior Directors state which outcomes they chose. Every one of these three moves inserts agency into a sentence that would otherwise be passive. "The migration is planned for cohort 3" has no person in it. "We did not migrate, and that was deliberate" has one.

There is also a credibility economy at work. Naming a non-result honestly, early, and without padding buys the reader's trust for the claims that follow. The block where you concede is what makes the blocks where you assert believable. Spend the concession deliberately, early, and short.

## How to apply

1. **Before writing, sort every gap into one of the three shapes.** Prevented loss, deliberate null, or slipped. Slipped then splits again into blocked and not-reached.
2. **Never assert the shape without the evidence in the next sentence.** A prevented-loss claim with no counterfactual and no proof of causation collapses under one question. Put the receipt immediately after the claim, not in an appendix.
3. **Keep the concession block short.** Length reads as anxiety. A short, flat block on the thing that did not happen reads as control.
4. **Do not announce your own confidence.** Stating a fact flatly is more confident than saying you are confident about it. Move any conviction language to *after* the evidence, as a closing line, and use it once per document.
5. **Check who in the audience wanted the opposite.** A prevented loss is only unambiguously good if nobody senior was pushing for speed. If someone was, pair the protection with evidence of movement in the same breath, and separate the two objects if they are genuinely different things.
