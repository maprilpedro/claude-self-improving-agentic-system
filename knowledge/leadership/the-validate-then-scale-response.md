# The Validate-Then-Scale Response

_Section: Executive Communication Patterns — part of `leadership/`; router = README.md._
- **Source**: Live observation — Bertrand/Pedro report-to-JIRA exchange, April 2, 2026
- **Date**: 2026-04-02
- **Insight**: When an executive asks a technical follow-up question on your work, the optimal response has three parts: (1) answer the specific question concisely and directly, (2) connect it to the already-agreed approach to show coherence — making their question look like it was anticipated, (3) signal forward momentum or scalability to show you're thinking beyond the current scope. This keeps you credible without over-explaining, and turns a clarifying question into a confirmation that you've thought further than they asked.
- **Anti-pattern**: Over-explaining the answer, defending the original decision, or treating the question as a challenge. Executives ask follow-up questions to learn, not to attack. Treat it as curiosity, not scrutiny.
- **PM Application**: Before any exec response to a technical question, ask: what's the one-sentence answer, how does it connect to what we already agreed, and what's the next horizon? Three sentences maximum. Bertrand's "pretty close from what I had in mind" after a short, direct response is the confirmation pattern to aim for.
