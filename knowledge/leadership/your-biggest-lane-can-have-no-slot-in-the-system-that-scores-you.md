# Your Biggest Lane Can Have No Slot in the System That Scores You

Date identified: 2026-07-21
Source: preparing Pedro's Apr→Jul 2026 check-in with Bertrand — cross-reading the captured goals (`Experience Hub - 2026 Yearly Review Goals.md`) against the OKR tree (`120 Projects/Work/OKRs/O1–O6`).

## The observation

Pedro's 2026 goals were captured as three statements, G1 reporting, G2 platform integration, G3 EH adoption. His OKRs were captured separately as six objectives, O1 through O6. Nobody ever mapped the two structures onto each other.

The result, measured on ~130 dated deliverables over four months:

- **G1 → O1.** Aligned.
- **G3 → O2.** Aligned.
- **G2 → nothing.** The Coworker migration absorbed ~70 deliverables, more than half the year's output, and has **no objective, no key result, no board column**.
- **O3, O4, O5, O6** map to no goal at all, and their KRs were quietly bulk-abandoned on 2026-07-13.

So the artifact a reviewer opens shows a PM who did some reporting and missed his EH adoption targets. The dominant lane, the one four separate organisations publicly named him to own, does not appear.

## Why this is not just "position over merit"

[[The Game Itself — Position Over Merit]] says good work does not get recognised on its own, and the fix is social, be present where decisions are made, claim the object, get named.

This is a different and sharper failure mode. Here the invisibility is **structural**. It would persist even with perfect self-promotion, because the container that leadership reads at review time has no field for the work. You can be named the owner in public by an architect, a PMM, a program manager and your own manager, and still be unscored, because naming happens in Slack and scoring happens in a form.

Merit and position are both about the work being seen. This is about the work being **countable**.

## The mechanism

New strategic work almost always arrives after the planning cycle that produced the goals. Goals get written in April. The programme that eats the year shows up in May. The goals are never reopened, because reopening them feels like moving the goalposts. So the biggest thing you did all year lands in the gap between two planning artifacts, and by review time the only honest place to put it is a narrative paragraph, which is exactly the format that does not survive comparison across candidates.

The tell, worth checking on any lane: **can I name the objective and key result this work rolls up to?** If the answer takes more than one sentence, the work is uncounted.

## How to apply

1. **Audit goal-to-OKR coverage at least once mid-cycle, not at review time.** Lay the goals against the objective list and look for the goal with no objective and the objective with no goal. Both are defects.
2. **When a new programme lands mid-cycle, open a container for it the same week.** An objective with two or three key results is enough. The point is not tracking discipline, it is that the work becomes addressable by the review process.
3. **If the cycle cannot be reopened, bring the mapping yourself.** Walk into the check-in with the goal-to-work mapping already drawn rather than letting the reviewer read a board that under-represents you. Framing this as "here is how my year maps to what we agreed" reads as ownership. Being asked "so what happened to G3" and answering from memory reads as drift.
4. **Read an abandoned-KR sweep as a signal, not housekeeping.** Bulk-abandoning key results without moving their substance anywhere is how a quarter of real work disappears from the record.
