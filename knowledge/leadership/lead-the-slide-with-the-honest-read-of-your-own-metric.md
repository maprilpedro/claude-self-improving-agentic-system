# Lead the Slide With the Honest Read of Your Own Metric, Not the Flattering Number

_Section: Executive Communication Patterns — part of `leadership/`; router = README.md._
- **Date identified**: 2026-06-07 · **promoted 2026-08-03** (System Review, 2 independent instances 7 weeks apart, different artifacts and audiences)
- **Source**:
  - **2026-06-07, Bucharest field-facing deck, slides 11/12/34.** Pedro killed two flattering-but-fragile headlines about his own product's data: *"Returning orgs pass 80%"* (a rate on a tiny base, where the truthful read is "few actually return") and *"the gap is new users, not value"* (which asserts value-is-proven, a claim the MAU peak-and-decline cannot carry). Replaced with *"early sign of stickiness, not proof at scale"* and *"too early to read value either way."* Delivered 06-10 to ~56 people including Bertrand; no push to re-inflate the numbers came back.
  - **2026-07-26/27, OKR-review speaking notes (KR 1e), in front of the leadership team.** Pedro enforced the same discipline three times unprompted inside one artifact: cut the external-vs-internal crossover milestone because the proving graph was not on the slide; caught *"companies come back, people do not"* because it leaned on a 65.5% the slide did not show, and rewrote it chart-backed only; and — *"on a toujours pas de preuve hein, donc aim to pas fixes"* — changed "the Coworker migration fixes this" to **"aims to fix"**, adding *"We will see in the retention numbers whether it works."*
  - Adjacent support, same discipline, 2026-07-24 (weekly KR status: retention bullet cut back to chart-backed only) and 2026-07-30 (release-notes paragraph: the "more finding types coming" line flagged as roadmap intent, not doc fact).
- **Insight**: The temptation in an exec artifact is not omission, it is **self-flattery about data you own**. A rate on a tiny base, a trend read off an unlabelled curve, a causal claim your own metric cannot carry — each looks like a win and each is the sentence a VP will challenge first. **Leading with the honest read costs nothing and buys three things**: it pre-empts the obvious challenge, it protects the credibility of every other number on the page, and in front of VPs it reads as *more* senior, not less. Think in probabilities, not certainties, applied to slide-building.
- **The strongest version of the move**: don't just hedge — **pre-announce the success criterion**. *"Aims to fix. We will see in the retention numbers whether it works"* converts an unprovable claim into a public, dated test that you have now defined. The hedge protects you; the pre-announced criterion positions you.
- **The test, before the artifact ships**: for every headline number, ask (1) is the evidence for this claim *on this slide*? (2) does the metric's unit actually support the causal verb I used? (3) if someone asks "how big is the base", does my number survive? If any answer is no, state the honest read instead — it is shorter anyway.
- **Distinct from**: [[Migrate Leadership From a Volume Metric to a Value Metric Without a Cliff]] (that changes which question the number answers; this keeps the question and refuses to overstate the answer) and [[Name the Shape of a Non-Result, or It Defaults to Failure]] (that is how to claim a thing that did not happen; this is how to report a thing that did, without inflating it).
- **3rd instance, 2026-08-07 — and it extends the entry from slides to a published dataset.** Rubin cannot group agent telemetry by the axis Pedro had chosen (the plugin) and offered the skill name instead, the axis his own decision had disqualified the same morning. He chose to publish on the name **with the measured disagreement rate attached** — the name resolves the grouping key cleanly on 51 of 98 rows, and one governance skill is provably filed under the wrong application by its own name. **Nobody asked him for that number.** Two things generalise beyond the slide case: (a) the honest read can be a *standing footnote on a recurring dataset*, not only a sentence in one artifact, and a footnote survives re-reads that a spoken caveat does not; (b) **the strongest form of the footnote is one named, checkable instance, not the percentage** — "51 of 98" invites a methodology argument, "this governance skill lands in Sites" ends it. ⚠️ The cost is that a number travels further than its caveat, so the footnote buys time rather than closing the gap.
