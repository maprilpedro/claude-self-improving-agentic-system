# Portfolio Investment Framing for Agent Headcount

_Section: Cross-Org Influence Without Authority — part of `leadership/`; router = README.md._
- **Date identified**: 2026-04-21
- **Source**: Jaclyn Eckersley in P42 Status meeting, April 21. Context: VP-level finance conversations are asking "how much HC is on agents across AEM?" The AEM Foundation answer (via Jaclyn): roll up individual team HC allocations at a percentage (20% of Foundation heads = on agents). Yanira asked Pedro to do the same for the agent portfolio at slide 40 of the planning deck. Rough estimates (50% of a person on agent work) are acceptable — exactness is not the point.
- **Pattern**: Senior leadership doesn't ask "how many people are on agents" expecting a headcount spreadsheet — they ask it to test whether you've modeled the investment as an investment. The portfolio framing (X% of Y teams = agent HC) has three advantages over raw counts: (1) it matches how finance thinks, so your numbers slot directly into their models; (2) it forces explicit acknowledgment of partial allocations (reality), which raw counts hide; (3) it makes your HC claim re-computable when team sizes or allocations shift. Raw counts go stale; percentages stay meaningful.
- **How to apply for agent HC rollup**: For each AEM agent, ask the PM owner: "Which teams are contributing? What percentage of each team's HC is on this agent?" Aggregate. Present as: "AEM agents represent X people across Y teams, averaging Z% allocation." Anchor your number against a known percentage (e.g., "Foundation allocates 20% — our agent HC is in line / above / below that baseline"). That anchor is what makes the number readable to a VP, not the raw total.
- **The second move — pair HC with revenue**: Jaclyn extended the frame: "at what point are we making money?" HC is the cost side. Without a matching revenue projection per agent, the FinOps conversation stays incomplete. Senior Director-level agent work has to pair HC investment with a monetization read — even if the monetization read is "unresolved, working with PMM." Don't walk into a VP with cost alone.
- **Anti-pattern**: Counting full-time-equivalents as raw integers. It looks precise, but it's usually wrong (real allocations are fractional) and it doesn't plug into finance models.
