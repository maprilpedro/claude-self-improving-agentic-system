# MOC-First Ship — Operationalization of Broadcast Frequency

_Section: Operating at Senior Director Level — part of `leadership/`; router = README.md._

- **Date**: 2026-05-08
- **Source**: Pedro 5-rep streak May 5-8 (Briefing v0 ✅ → AOv2 5-phase plan ✅ → MCP MOC ✅ → NYL recovery → Master-list scoping group DM ✅ → all with leader 1-click ack within hours).
- **Insight**: The "broadcast frequency" muscle becomes operational through a specific artifact-shape pattern: **ship a tangible MOC (mockup, draft, scope outline) before requirements are complete, before data is connected, before the artifact is "polished" — and use the senior leader's 1-click ack as direction validation.** Then gate broader broadcast on internal polish. The pattern compresses what would normally be a 2-3 week alignment cycle into a 24-hour direction-check.
- **The four ingredients that make it work**:
  1. **Tangible artifact (not a description of one).** Slack a deck snapshot, a mockup, a 1-pager, a draft Slack message — never "I'm thinking about building X." The thing itself, even if 30% complete.
  2. **Honest caveat in the cover note.** Examples: *"Still refining on my side, but gives you the direction."* / *"Numbers Claude invented for the report template, not connected, not validated."* / *"Draft for review."* Caveats protect the relationship while the work is rough.
  3. **Direct send to the senior leader, not the team.** Bertrand-direct beats team-broadcast for direction-validation. Team broadcast is for stage 3 (after polish).
  4. **One specific signal sought.** Not approval. Not feedback. **Direction** — *"is this the shape you wanted?"* Leader's 1-click reaction (👍, "go ahead", "looks right") = direction confirmed. Anything more = signal for revision.
- **Why this compresses the cycle**: Without the MOC, leaders have to imagine what you're building from your verbal description, then ask clarifying questions, then wait for a draft, then react. With the MOC, the imagining step is eliminated — they react to the artifact, not the idea. **The artifact does the cognitive work the conversation would have done.**
- **The gating rule for broader broadcast**: After 1-click ack, the artifact still needs to be polished before it goes to the team / agent owners / cross-org. The leader sign-off is direction-grade, not publication-grade. Treat them as separate stages: stage 1 ship to leader (rough OK), stage 2 polish, stage 3 broadcast wide.

- **Counter-pattern — what doesn't work**:
  - Pitch a structure verbally without a MOC → leaders ask "where is it?" and you lose the moment.
  - Wait for full polish before showing → cycle stretches to weeks, leader's mind moves on.
  - Send to multiple people simultaneously → no one reads carefully because anyone could.
- **Why this is Senior Director, not Director**: Directors execute against approved specs. Senior Directors ship MOCs that *create* the spec by giving leaders something to react to. The PM who ships the MOC defines the artifact. The PM who waits for the spec executes someone else's artifact.
- **PM application**: Build a "MOC drawer" — at any moment have 2-3 half-finished artifacts ready to send when the right moment arrives. The half-finished state IS the value, not a defect. The artifact that's polished enough to publish is too late to use as direction-check.
