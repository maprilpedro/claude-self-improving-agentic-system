# Govern a Consistency Layer Over Primitives You Don't Own

_Section: Cross-Org Influence Without Authority — part of `leadership/`; router = README.md._
- **Date identified**: 2026-07-01 (promoted from parked candidate — 2 independent instances: 2026-06-04, 2026-06-16)
- **Source**: (1) 2026-06-04 Silvia / Ian / Eugene call — Pedro can own the cross-surface consistency *definition* + the *check*, but the shared chat primitives (chat window, side rail, "+") are built by the **AO-two UX team**. (2) 2026-06-16 Coworker skills read — AEM's assistant identity in Coworker is governed by `cx-coworker-base` (AEP-owned defaults: quick_actions, brand blue, system_prompt) and the manifest lives in AEP's repo (`push:false`). Two distinct dates, surfaces, owning teams, and technical objects; same structural insight.
- **Pattern**: You can be handed ownership of a consistency layer (a cross-surface standard, a selection UX, a declaration format) while the **primitives that layer governs are owned by another team**. The layer then governs a dependency it doesn't fully control. The trap is to treat the ownership as complete and get blocked later when the unowned primitive can't be changed (AO v1 lesson per Silvia: a file-upload dependency couldn't be changed without editing AO v1 UI code → roadmap → Summit → never done). The move is to **name the seam early** — who owns the common library, how governance across the seam happens, what you decide vs what you consume — so the layer isn't silently hostage to an unowned base.
- **Why it's a Senior Director move**: owning "definition + check + the cross-team governance seam" is a more durable and more senior claim than owning the components. Components get rebuilt; the governance seam is where your layer's authority actually lives. Naming the seam out loud (rather than assuming the primitives are yours) reads as system-level thinking, not territorial reach.
- **Distinct from "Definition Ownership Is the Moat"**: that entry is about **data** (who counts as a user, entity lists, filtering). This is about **UI/assembly primitives** (chat rail, manifest base, shared component library) and the cross-team governance dependency they create. Adjacent, not duplicate.
- **Anti-pattern**: claiming the consistency layer as fully owned, skipping the who-owns-the-base question, then discovering at build time that the primitive can't move without another team's roadmap. Or the inverse over-reach — trying to annex the primitives themselves instead of governing the seam.
- **Caveat (evidence grade)**: both instances are Pedro's structural reads, not yet outcome-validated. Watch for an outcome instance (the seam actually blocking or unblocking a deliverable) to harden it.
