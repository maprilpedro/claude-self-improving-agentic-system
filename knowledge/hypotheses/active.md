## H-010: Per-User Credit Attribution and Access Control Will Gate Coworker Expansion at Enterprise AEM Customers Before Skill Quality Does

- **Status**: Testing — opened 2026-09-22 (consolidation, from the 09-16 → 09-22 Slack audit and the Esri email thread).
- **Date proposed**: 2026-09-22
- **Category**: AI-product / measurement (the reporting layer Pedro owns, G1; Senior Director scope lens via [[reference_customer_esri]])
- **Hypothesis**: the ask that blocks broader Coworker use at large AEM customers is not "does the skill work" but "who spent which credits on what, and can I restrict who may". Until per-user, per-action attribution and program-level access control exist, credit purchases stay experimental and admins compare Adobe unfavourably with Copilot's justify / monitor / audit / disable model.
- **Evidence for**: four independent customers in three weeks — VW and Centene (Mike Tilburg, 09-02, per-user credit visibility, "not possible today"); Esri (Jeff Troccoli via Pierre Tager, 09-15, 91% of credits, a ~4,000-credit spike nobody can attribute, the Copilot comparison; Corey 09-19 "continued concerns"); UHG and Capital Group (Nina Mokoosio, 09-17, program- or user-group-level access control → `AEMAGT-2818`); Infineon (Fabian Lotz, 09-21/22, how credits are computed for multi-step tasks). Bertrand 09-20: "this must be a very generic ask across all Coworker customers."
- **Evidence for, 2026-09-27 — the first DECISION, not an ask (1 of the 3 needed):** Workday opted out of Coworker. Reason stated by the customer on the 09-15 call (Pedro + Tina with Workday's architect and project manager, first-hand): they must be able to control who uses Coworker, what it can access, read vs write. Return condition not stated. Same customer is the #1 champion on AEM MCP direct (6,607 ops since June, 99.9% Cursor, user OAuth), so the opt-out is from the ungovernable surface, not from AI on AEM (inference). Source: Pedro, 2026-09-27; [[reference_customer_workday]].
- **Evidence, 2026-10-13 (PTO Slack audit 09-28 → 10-12), asks not decisions, count unchanged at 1 of 3:** governance asks recur from Intuit (audit of MCP actions) and FSI clients refusing any AI-created content; Clint (10-05) asked how access is controlled. **First move on arm (a):** the 10-15 Coworker release gives user-level access management to *new* customers only; existing customers need a migration, no date given. Watch whether Workday or Esri cite it. Martin Buergi (10-01) reads the blocker as org-level rather than user-level, which supports the access-control arm over the attribution arm. Source: Slack, [[project_aem_agents_intelligence]] 2026-10-13 block.
- **Evidence, 2026-10-14 (consolidation of the 10-13 opt-out reasons list, 59 lines, pasted by Pedro, origin not stated), decisions, access-control arm now at 3 named:** BofA ("eager to adopt Coworker … blocked … until either user-level permissions or sandbox restrictions for Coworker are available") and Merck ("inability to restrict rollout based on user or group") opted out on control, joining Workday. ## H-011: A User Whose AEM Chat Ends on a Permission Block Does Not Come Back

- **Status**: Testing — opened 2026-10-14 (brainstorm on Coworker AEM adoption).
- **Date proposed**: 2026-10-14
- **Category**: AI-product / adoption measurement (G1 reporting lane; feeds the adoption plan for Bertrand and Loni)
- **Hypothesis** (Pedro's, verbatim intent: "il travaille beaucoup, et au moment de commit/executer, il a une limite de droits, il ne va pas revenir"): users whose AEM Coworker chat ends on an org permission or process block return the following week at a materially lower rate than users whose chat resolved.
- **Motivating signal (not evidence; relabelled at the 10-14 review, nothing here measures return)**: Rubin Quality VA7 7-day windows 10-13/10-14 (screenshots, Pedro): AEM chats resolve 54% vs 70% across Coworker; org-permission blocks ~14% of AEM chats vs ~3%; abandonment among unresolved 31% AEM vs 19% Coworker. Newell Brands: 32 turns ending on "[Permission requested for: api_request]". Weekly retention 160 of 576 users (Rubin weekly report, 10-14). All indirect: no user-level return data yet.
- **Evidence against**: Rubin labels 32 of 33 abandons as user-cancelled, not system failures; AEM Sites Trial's 403 came after 4 turns, not after long work; sample is one region, ~112 chats.
- **Test / resolution**: by **2026-11-30**, once Rubin access is restored (legal hold since 09-25): for the AEM chats labelled "org permissions or process", check whether the same user opened a Coworker chat in the following 7 days; compare with users of resolved chats. **Confirm** if the blocked group's return rate is half or less of the resolved group's on 30+ blocked chats. **Kill** if the gap is under ~10 points. **Inconclusive, not killed,** if the data cannot be split by user or blocked chats stay under 30 (VA7 alone shows 16, so the test needs several regions, which needs the legal hold lifted). **Between the bars** (e.g. 40% vs 25%): keep testing one more cycle. **Control** for user tenure and org type (trial or new users may return less anyway). *Fixes from the 10-14 System Review (`promotion-judge`).*
- **If confirmed**: permission blocks are an adoption leak, not just a quality metric; the handoff fix outranks value promotion in the plan.
- **If disconfirmed**: blocked users come back anyway; the permission fix is a quality item, and the value story carries the +20%.
