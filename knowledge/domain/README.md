# PM Domain Knowledge

> Core product management knowledge organized by practice area.

## Product Discovery

### Sell the Dream, Not the Widget
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Prologue
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Insight**: Jobs never sold computers. He sold tools to unleash human potential. Starbucks doesn't sell coffee, it sells a "third place." The most inspiring communicators sell a vision of a better life, not the product itself. Ask: "What am I really selling?"
- **Implication**: Product discovery should start with the transformation your product enables, not feature specs. Every product brief should answer "What dream does this fulfill?" before listing requirements.

### Start with the Customer Experience, Work Backward to Technology
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 2 (Jobs quote, WWDC 1997)
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Insight**: Jobs's approach was always to define the experience first, then figure out the technology. When launching iMac (1998), he described the user need (get on the Internet simply and fast) before any technical specs.
- **Implication**: Discovery frameworks should begin with desired experience states, not feature brainstorms. The "why should I care?" question should be answerable before any technical scoping begins.

### Passion as Product Signal
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 3
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Insight**: Jobs, Schultz (Starbucks), Gardner all point to the same thing: the best products emerge when builders are genuinely passionate about the problem. Jobs: "I never did it for the money." Collins & Porras (Built to Last) confirmed: visionary companies are driven by "core values and sense of purpose beyond just making money."
- **Implication**: When evaluating product ideas or team assignments, founder/team passion for the problem space is a leading indicator of success. If nobody on the team is personally annoyed by the problem, reconsider the investment.

## User Personas & Segments

### Three Types of Learners (Audience Segmentation)
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 12
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Characteristics**: Visual (40% of people, learn by seeing), Auditory (20-30%, learn by listening), Kinesthetic (learn by doing/touching)
- **Needs**: Visual learners need images, not text. Auditory learners need stories and verbal techniques. Kinesthetic learners need hands-on interaction.
- **Signals**: Visual learners respond to diagrams and screenshots. Auditory learners engage with narrative. Kinesthetic learners want demos and prototypes.

## Competitive Landscape

### Positioning Against Competitors Through Problem Framing
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 6
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Signal**: Jobs consistently framed competitors as "the problem" before positioning Apple as the solution. iPhone launch: showed four existing smartphones, highlighted their fixed keyboards as the fundamental flaw, then introduced touch. The competitor became the pain point.
- **Implication**: Competitive positioning is most powerful when you define the problem in terms that make competitors look structurally unable to solve it. Don't compare features. Redefine the category.

## Market Signals

### Word of Mouth Is the #1 Purchase Influencer
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 1
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Signal**: Customer evidence and third-party endorsements are the most effective sales tools. Jobs consistently used media quotes, partner testimonials, and customer stories in launches.
- **Strength**: Strong
- **Timeframe**: Permanent

## Metrics & Analytics

### Adoption Metrics Should Be Ratios, Not Absolutes
- **Source**: Bertrand de Coatpont (Sr Director PM, Adobe AEM), Slack exchange April 2, 2026
- **Date**: 2026-04-02
- **Insight**: When measuring adoption of a new channel or modality (e.g. MCP tool usage), the meaningful metric is the ratio against baseline behavior — not the absolute count. "How many content edits via MCP vs. via the UI" is more useful than "how many MCP calls." The ratio shows displacement and real adoption; the absolute number just shows activity.
- **Application**: When proposing metrics for new AI or agent features, always pair the new channel metric with the existing baseline metric. Ratios tell the adoption story. Absolutes don't.

### Numbers Without Context Are Meaningless
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 9
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Insight**: 5GB means nothing. "1,000 songs in your pocket" changes behavior. Jobs made every number specific (exact quantities), relevant (to audience's life), and contextual (compared to familiar things). Apple's 5% market share became meaningful when compared to BMW/Mercedes.
- **Application**: Every metric in a presentation or report should pass three tests: Is it specific? Is it relevant to this audience? Is there context (analogy, comparison) that makes it meaningful? Raw numbers without these are wasted slides.

### The 10-Minute Attention Rule
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Intermission 1 (John Medina research)
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Insight**: Peer-reviewed studies confirm audiences check out at exactly 10 minutes. The brain follows "stubborn timing patterns." Jobs never went more than 10 minutes without a change: demo, video, guest speaker, or new topic.
- **Application**: Structure any presentation, meeting, or review to introduce a shift every 10 minutes. This is not optional. It's neuroscience.

## Stakeholder Management

### The One Question That Matters: "Why Should I Care?"
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 2
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Context**: Every pitch, presentation, product review, or stakeholder update.
- **Approach**: Before any technical detail, answer the audience's unspoken question: "Why should I care?" Jobs always led with the benefit, not the how. When Intel switched to 64-bit, Gates explained the technical process. Jobs said: "It was a huge heart transplant. We did it in seven months."
- **Anti-pattern**: Starting with technical specs, org chart changes, or process details before establishing why the audience should pay attention.

### Eliminate Jargon to Increase Influence
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 10
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Context**: Any communication with executives, cross-functional partners, or customers.
- **Approach**: Jobs's language scored dramatically better than Gates on every readability metric: fewer words per sentence (10.5 vs 21.6), lower lexical density (16.5% vs 21%), fewer hard words (2.9% vs 5.1%), lower fog index (5.5 vs 10.7). Use simple, concrete, emotional words. "Amazingly zippy" beats "dramatically improved network throughput."
- **Anti-pattern**: Using buzzwords like "synergy," "best of breed," "leverage" to sound smart. Jack Welch: "Insecure managers create complexity." Suze Orman: "It's our fear of not being important that leads us to communicate in a more complex way than we need to."

### Share the Stage to Build Coalition
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 11
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Context**: Major product launches, executive reviews, team presentations.
- **Approach**: Jobs consistently brought partners (Intel CEO in bunny suit), employees (Jony Ive for design), media quotes, and customer testimonials into presentations. A partner physically present is more powerful than a reference. Credit employees publicly. Thank customers.
- **Anti-pattern**: Making every presentation a one-person show. Failing to acknowledge the team. Hogging the spotlight.

### Rally People to a Better Future
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 3 (Buckingham, *The One Thing You Need to Know*)
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Context**: Leadership communication, vision-setting, organizational change.
- **Approach**: Marcus Buckingham's definition of leadership after interviewing thousands of peak performers: "Great leaders rally people to a better future." A leader carries a vivid image of what the future could be and is "never satisfied with the present, because in your head you can see a better future, and the friction between 'what is' and 'what could be' burns you."
- **Anti-pattern**: Communicating only about current state or problems without painting a vivid picture of the desired future.

### Dress for the Leader You Want to Become
- **Source**: Gallo, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 16
- **Date**: 2026-03-19
- **Context**: Any visible leadership moment.
- **Approach**: Jobs wore suits to the bank and jeans in the office. Army Ranger Matt Eversmann: "Great leaders dress a little better than everyone else." Men's Wearhouse founder George Zimmer added: "appropriate for the culture." Your wardrobe signals the role you're stepping into.
- **Anti-pattern**: Dressing down for important moments. Or dressing way above the culture norms in a way that creates distance.

## Coalition Building & Alliances (from Greene, *The 33 Strategies of War*)

### Build Networks That Have Mutual Interest, Not Just Loyalty
- **Source**: Greene, *The 33 Strategies of War*, Strategy 27: The Alliance Strategy
- **Date**: 2026-04-02
- **Insight**: The best alliances are not built on personal loyalty but on aligned self-interest. When people stay in a coalition because they personally gain from it — not just because they like you — the coalition is durable. Loyalty fades; self-interest is more reliable. The strategist's job is to identify the overlapping interests and make them explicit.
- **Application**: Before any cross-org initiative, map what each key stakeholder actually wants. Not what they say they want in a roadmap session — what they care about in terms of their own goals, their team's visibility, their org's metrics. Find the genuine overlap. Make your ask feel like it helps them get what they were already trying to get.

### Sow Dissension in Opposing Coalitions by Isolating Members
- **Source**: Greene, *The 33 Strategies of War*, Strategy 17: Defeat Them in Detail (The Divide-and-Conquer Strategy)
- **Date**: 2026-04-02
- **Insight**: When facing a large, unified opposing force — whether a competing team, a hostile coalition, or an org that has aligned against your initiative — the worst strategy is a frontal attack. The better approach is to identify the weakest member or the most detachable component and work on that first. By separating the parts and addressing them individually, a formidable unified force becomes a series of manageable individual conversations.
- **Application**: When a cross-org initiative faces broad resistance, don't try to change everyone's mind at once. Find the one stakeholder who is closest to agreeable and win them first. Use that win as social proof for the next. The sequence matters: easiest first, hardest last. Each conversion changes the math for everyone who hasn't yet been won.

### Occupy the Moral High Ground — The Righteous Strategy
- **Source**: Greene, *The 33 Strategies of War*, Strategy 25: Occupy the Moral High Ground (The Righteous Strategy)
- **Date**: 2026-04-02
- **Insight**: In political and organizational environments, the side that appears to be fighting for a just cause has a structural advantage. It is harder to attack someone who clearly holds the moral or principled position. By framing your work in terms of what's right for the customer, the user, the team — not what's right for you — you make it costly for opponents to attack without appearing self-interested.
- **Application**: Frame every major initiative in terms of user benefit and company mission, not personal/team wins. "This is what our customers need" is harder to argue against than "I think we should do this." When under organizational attack, don't respond defensively — hold the principle. The person defending the principled position earns the benefit of the doubt from observers who are undecided.

### Read Behavior, Not Intent — Judge People by Their Actions
- **Source**: Greene, *The 33 Strategies of War*, Preface and Strategy 1 (Keys to Warfare section)
- **Date**: 2026-04-02
- **Insight**: Greene's preface establishes a foundational principle: "What people say about themselves does not matter; people will say anything. Look at what they have done; deeds do not lie." In organizations especially, where political maneuvering is disguised as collaboration and allies sometimes work against you indirectly, the ability to read behavior patterns rather than stated intent is a critical skill.
- **Application**: When evaluating whether a stakeholder is genuinely aligned or performing alignment, watch the pattern of their actions over 3-4 weeks. Do their decisions consistently support the shared goal, or do small moves consistently disadvantage your initiative while their stated support stays warm? The pattern tells the truth. Mao detected Lin Biao's plotting not from evidence of conspiracy but from a change in behavior — effusive praise that was too fervent. Register changes in emotional temperature. That is where the signal is.
