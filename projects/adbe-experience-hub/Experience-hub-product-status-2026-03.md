# Experience Hub — Product Status, March 2026

*New PM read. Data from ~91 Adobe Analytics (CJA) dashboard screenshots taken March 20, 2026. GA was August 26, 2025 — 7 months post-launch.*

---

## State of the product

Experience Hub is a functioning home screen that has not yet become the AI front door it is supposed to be. The base product works: ~4,930 weekly active users, a 70%+ repeat rate, and reasonable engagement with Navigation and Quick Actions. Users who land on Experience Hub come back. That is genuinely good for a 7-month-old product sitting on top of an established AEM user base with its own inertia.

But the AI story — the actual strategic bet — is not landing. Agent usage across all tracked AI features sits at 0.04%–0.06%. AI Capabilities (Storage Hints, handcrafted prompts) have sub-1% engagement. If the goal is to make Experience Hub the surface where AEM agents get adopted at scale, the current data says we are not close.

The gap between base product health and AI feature engagement is the defining signal right now.

---

## What the numbers say

**Weekly Active Users: ~4,930**
Healthy baseline. Users who come, return — 70%+ repeat rate. Navigation and Quick Actions have 10%–60% engagement across personas. The home screen is doing its job.

**Adoption funnel: ~20–30% of AEM base is active**
Only a fraction of the full AEM user base has adopted Experience Hub at any level, 7 months post-GA. The product has been exposed to ~60–70% of the base but most have not converted to active use.

**AI/Agent usage: near zero**
AI Agents Roles behavior at 0.04%–0.06%. "Events since GA" for agent interactions: 21 (Jun–Sep 2025). AI Capabilities features under 1% engagement. These are not slow-start numbers — they signal something fundamental is broken in the AI discovery and delivery path.

## Persona breakdown — who is and is not using it

The dashboard tracks three persona segments across Navigation, Quick Actions, and WAU:

| Persona | Share of navigation events | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Content Authors | ~56% | Only persona with meaningful usage |
| Developers | ~5% | Marginal |
| Assets Admin / Asset Librarians | ~3% | Marginal |
| System Administrators | Not tracked | Invisible |
| Business Owners | Not tracked | Invisible |

**The product has become a Content Author tool.** The four other target personas are either marginal or not measurable. This is a strategic problem because the agents targeted at those personas — Developer Agent, Discovery, Optimization, Governance — need those users in the product to matter.

## The four priorities

### 1. Fix agents and make prompts reliable

This is the core bet. With 40–50% agent failure rates and near-zero agent engagement, the product cannot drive AI adoption until the experience works. This is not a marketing or distribution problem — 4,900+ weekly users are already in the product. It is a quality and discovery problem.

The sequence: fix agent quality → make the prompt interaction feel reliable → use Experience Hub's existing reach (70%+ repeat, active navigation) to drive agent discovery at scale.

### 2. Reset the vision-reality gap before Summit

New PM window. Stakeholders believe features are live that are not. Leadership may have seen a mock demo, not the actual product. Every month this goes unaddressed, you inherit the previous PM's credibility problem. Summit is roughly one month out.

The move: name the gap clearly, reset expectations honestly, present a realistic scope. A new PM who does this is demonstrating Senior Director judgment — the ability to say no and hold the line is exactly what separates Directors from Senior Directors at this level.

### 3. Get Developers and Asset Librarians into the product

Fixing agents for Content Authors only is a half-win. The agents that matter for AEM's differentiation story — Developer Agent, Discovery, Optimization, Governance — need Developers, Asset Librarians, System Admins, and Business Owners to show up. They currently do not.

The question to answer: why is Experience Hub irrelevant to a Developer today? Is it the widgets shown? The navigation? The agent not working? That answer drives what gets built next.

### 4. Build a contribution model

Two engineers, 15+ teams asking for things. Without a process for how other teams build and ship their own widgets inside Experience Hub, the team stays a service desk and the backlog never shrinks. This is also the structural case for headcount — you cannot argue for more resources without a model showing how they would be leveraged.

Target: define what "contributing to Experience Hub" looks like and make it real for one external team before end of Q2.

## What to track weekly

1. WAU — primary health check, currently ~4,930
2. Repeat user rate — currently 70%+, measures stickiness
3. Adoption funnel penetration — % of AEM base active at any level
4. AI/Agent interaction count — the strategic forward-looking metric, currently near zero
5. Navigation depth — are users going beyond the landing screen

---

## What to skip

- Per-shortcut click breakdowns
- Individual submitted prompt text analysis
- Raw announcement event counts
- Per-persona navigation granularity (useful for UX, not exec visibility)

---

## Story to tell upward

Experience Hub has a working base. Users come and return. The home screen does what it should. That is a credible foundation.

The next chapter — agents through Experience Hub — does not have evidence yet. Seven months post-GA, agent interactions are in the dozens. This is not a distribution problem. It is a quality and experience problem. Agents fail 40–50% of the time and the prompt discovery path is not obvious.

The honest story: the product is the front door. The AI rooms inside the house are not ready for guests. The work for 2026 is finishing the rooms, resetting what stakeholders expect to find, and then using Experience Hub's reach to drive agent adoption at scale.

This is visible work. Experience Hub sits at the intersection of three things leadership cares about — AI agents, cross-team platform strategy, and AEM Cloud adoption. Getting this right is a Senior Director-level opportunity.
